# Montage study: an eighth motor electrode, and blinks without Fp1

Montage A uses eight electrodes: seven over the motor cortex for decoding
(`FC3, FC4, C3, Cz, C4, CP3, CP4`) and `Fp1`, above the left eye, for the
double blink that triggers `action`. Two questions were measured on public
data before changing it:

1. Which central electrode best completes the seven decoding electrodes, in
   particular for `forward` (feet imagery, the weakest command)?
2. On a headset with only eight channels, can the double blink be detected on
   `FC3` and `FC4` instead of `Fp1`, freeing a channel for the motor cortex?

Tables: [`research/results/montage.md`](../research/results/montage.md),
regenerated by `uv run python research/experiments/montage.py`.

## 1. The eighth electrode: CPz

Candidates `FCz`, `CPz`, `C1`, `C2`, each added to the seven decoding
electrodes; `csp-lda` and `ts-lr`; left, right, forward; BNCI2014-001
(9 subjects) and PhysionetMI (39 of subjects 1-50, the others having too few
clean epochs).

| Candidate | Δ accuracy (10 cells) | Δ `forward` recall, new user (4 cells) |
|---|---|---|
| **CPz** | **+0.2 to +2.6 points, positive in 10 of 10** | **+3.0 to +7.3 points** |
| FCz | -1.2 to +1.6, mixed | -2.4 to +5.2, mixed |
| C1 | -1.7 to +0.3, mostly negative | -2.2 to +0.9 |
| C2 | -1.6 to +0.3, mostly negative | -2.0 to +2.4 |

A cell is one dataset × protocol × model; Δ is the mean paired difference
with the seven electrodes alone.

- **CPz is the only candidate that helps consistently**: every one of its ten
  accuracy differences is positive, and it improves the recognition of
  `forward` for a new user by 3 to 7 points on both datasets. CPz lies behind Cz
  on the midline, over the feet area of the sensorimotor cortex.
- The gains are **small**: about +1 point of overall accuracy, larger on the
  command that needed it. Taken one by one, few differences reach p < 0.05
  (Wilcoxon, 9 or 39 subjects, no correction for multiple comparisons); the
  argument is the consistency of the sign across two datasets, three protocols
  and two models.
- C1 and C2 add nothing: they are too close to C3 and C4, already present.

**Decision**: the eighth motor electrode is **CPz**. The decoding montage
becomes `FC3, FC4, C3, Cz, C4, CP3, CP4, CPz`.

## 2. Blinks without Fp1: not reliable

On 617 minutes of PhysionetMI recordings (50 subjects), blinks detected on Fp1
served as the reference; the same detector then ran on FC3 and FC4.

- A blink reaches FC3/FC4 with about **a third of its Fp1 amplitude** (median
  0.32), while the slow background there is of the order of 20 µV (19 µV robust
  deviation, measured on 6 subjects during exploration): a 100-300 µV blink
  becomes 30-100 µV, barely above it.
- No setting works. Keeping false `action` commands under 10 per hour finds
  only a quarter of the blinks; finding three quarters of them or more gives 36
  to 160 false `action` commands per hour. Requiring FC3 and FC4 to rise together
  (the blink is symmetric) helps a little, not enough.

**Decision**: the double blink stays on **Fp1**. A headset without Fp1 has no
eye-based `action` command.

## 3. A problem with the double blink itself

The study revealed a larger issue: **people double-blink on their own**. Over
the same recordings, with no instruction to blink, the detector finds 47
spontaneous double blinks per hour on Fp1 (in an exploratory count per subject:
median 29 per hour, only 4 of 50 subjects with none). Triple blinks still occur 4 to 8 times per hour. The
detector had only been validated on synthetic signals without spontaneous
blinks, where it gave no false detection.

As specified, `action` would therefore fire on its own every one or two
minutes. Before it drives a real object, `action` needs one or more of:

- **context**: `action` only acts where it makes sense (in front of a door),
  which sandbox already plans;
- **a less natural gesture**: a triple blink, or a long deliberate eye closure;
- **per-user amplitude calibration**: deliberate blinks are usually much
  stronger than spontaneous ones, which the calibration session can measure;
- **confirmation** for actions that matter.

These options need recordings of deliberate blinks, which public
motor-imagery datasets do not contain; they will be measured with the headset
(phase B).

## Resulting montages

| Headset | Decoding (8) | `action` |
|---|---|---|
| 9 channels or more | `FC3, FC4, C3, Cz, C4, CP3, CP4, CPz` | Fp1 |
| 8 channels (IronBCI-8), choice 1 | the seven of montage A | Fp1 |
| 8 channels (IronBCI-8), choice 2 | the eight above | none from the eyes |

Choice 1 keeps `action`; choice 2 gains about a point of accuracy and a few
points on `forward`. Models trained on the eight decoding electrodes cannot run
on seven, and the reverse: each choice needs its own set of models.

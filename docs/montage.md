# Montage study: an eighth motor electrode, and blinks without Fp1

Montage A uses eight electrodes: seven over the motor cortex for decoding
(`FC3, FC4, C3, Cz, C4, CP3, CP4`) and `Fp1`, above the left eye, for the
double blink that triggers `action`. Two questions were measured on public
data before changing it:

1. Which central electrode best completes the seven decoding electrodes, in
   particular for `forward` (feet imagery, the weakest command)?
2. On a headset with only eight channels, can the double blink be detected on
   `FC3` and `FC4` instead of `Fp1`, freeing a channel for the motor cortex?

Tables: [`research/results/montage.md`](../research/results/montage.md),
regenerated by `uv run python research/experiments/montage.py`.

## 1. The eighth electrode: CPz

Candidates `FCz`, `CPz`, `C1`, `C2`, each added to the seven decoding
electrodes; `csp-lda` and `ts-lr`; left, right, forward; BNCI2014-001
(9 subjects) and PhysionetMI (39 of subjects 1-50, the others having too few
clean epochs).

| Candidate | Δ accuracy (10 cells) | Δ `forward` recall, new user (4 cells) |
|---|---|---|
| **CPz** | **+0.2 to +2.6 points, positive in 10 of 10** | **+3.0 to +7.3 points** |
| FCz | -1.2 to +1.6, mixed | -2.4 to +5.2, mixed |
| C1 | -1.7 to +0.3, mostly negative | -2.2 to +0.9 |
| C2 | -1.6 to +0.3, mostly negative | -2.0 to +2.4 |

A cell is one dataset × protocol × model; Δ is the mean paired difference
with the seven electrodes alone.

- **CPz is the only candidate that helps consistently**: every one of its ten
  accuracy differences is positive, and it improves the recognition of
  `forward` for a new user by 3 to 7 points on both datasets. CPz lies behind Cz
  on the midline, over the feet area of the sensorimotor cortex.
- The gains are **small**: about +1 point of overall accuracy, larger on the
  command that needed it. Taken one by one, few differences reach p < 0.05
  (Wilcoxon, 9 or 39 subjects, no correction for multiple comparisons); the
  argument is the consistency of the sign across two datasets, three protocols
  and two models.
- C1 and C2 add nothing: they are too close to C3 and C4, already present.

**Decision**: the eighth motor electrode is **CPz**. The decoding montage
becomes `FC3, FC4, C3, Cz, C4, CP3, CP4, CPz`.

### Retrained decoders on montage C

All baselines and deep models were retrained on the eight motor electrodes,
with the same datasets, protocols, seed and training recipe as on montage A
(full tables: [`research/results/montage-c.md`](../research/results/montage-c.md),
to compare with [`research/results/deep.md`](../research/results/deep.md)).
Mean accuracy in %, montage C (montage A):

| Dataset, classes | Protocol | `csp-lda` | `ts-lr` | `eegnet` | `shallow` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BNCI2014-001, left/right | within-session | 77.2 (76.8) | 74.9 (74.8) | 66.4 (68.6) | 68.6 (68.9) |
| | cross-subject | 67.7 (68.8) | 71.0 (72.3) | 71.9 (73.7) | 67.7 (67.8) |
| BNCI2014-001, left/right/forward | within-session | **70.2** (68.3) | 69.2 (67.8) | 56.4 (61.4) | 61.3 (61.5) |
| | cross-session | **67.8** (65.6) | 65.6 (64.9) | 58.5 (62.4) | 61.8 (60.2) |
| | cross-subject | 53.5 (53.2) | 54.8 (52.2) | **55.0** (52.8) | 50.3 (49.7) |
| PhysionetMI, left/right | within-session | 60.8 (60.5) | 60.0 (58.7) | 50.9 (52.4) | 50.9 (56.5) |
| | cross-subject | 62.9 (63.1) | 59.8 (62.8) | 54.4 (59.6) | 51.1 (53.0) |

- **Left/right: no change** (within one or two points), as expected: CPz lies
  on the midline, over the feet area.
- **Three classes: the gain is confirmed** for the classical decoders (+1 to
  +2.6 points, the same numbers as in the study above) and EEGNet becomes the
  best decoder for a new user (55.0 %).
- **EEGNet's losses are training failures, not a montage effect.** Within a
  session (about 170 training epochs), most sessions score the same on both
  montages, but EEGNet sometimes fails to learn and stays near chance (subject
  1, first session: 79 % on montage A, 51 % on C; 87 % on C for its second
  session). On PhysionetMI, with about 35 epochs per subject, the networks are
  near chance on both montages. Small-data training of deep networks is
  unstable: fast calibration (`CORE-060`) will start from pre-trained weights,
  and should average several seeds before drawing conclusions.
- The six models pre-trained on montage C are in the registry
  (`<eegnet|shallow>-<experiment>-c`): the starting points of fast calibration
  for the IronBCI-8.

## 2. Blinks without Fp1: not reliable

On 617 minutes of PhysionetMI recordings (50 subjects), blinks detected on Fp1
served as the reference; the same detector then ran on FC3 and FC4.

- A blink reaches FC3/FC4 with about **a third of its Fp1 amplitude** (median
  0.32), while the slow background there is of the order of 20 µV (19 µV robust
  deviation, measured on 6 subjects during exploration): a 100-300 µV blink
  becomes 30-100 µV, barely above it.
- No setting works. Keeping false `action` commands under 10 per hour finds
  only a quarter of the blinks; finding three quarters of them or more gives 36
  to 160 false `action` commands per hour. Requiring FC3 and FC4 to rise together
  (the blink is symmetric) helps a little, not enough.

- Combining the **eight motor electrodes** does better, not enough. A spatial
  filter learned from Fp1-labelled blinks (weights positive at the front,
  negative at the back, as the blink topography predicts) finds 35 % of the
  strong blinks when learned on other subjects, 45 % when learned on the user's
  own first runs, with 10 to 13 false `action` commands per hour. A plain
  front-minus-back difference does no better than FC3/FC4.

**Decision**: blinks can only be detected reliably on **Fp1**. With eight motor
electrodes and no Fp1, the eyes cannot trigger `action`.

## 3. The double blink is not a good command

The study revealed a larger issue: **people double-blink on their own**. Over
the same recordings, with no instruction to blink, the detector finds 47
spontaneous double blinks per hour on Fp1 (in an exploratory count per subject:
median 29 per hour, only 4 of 50 subjects with none). Triple blinks still occur
4 to 8 times per hour. The detector had only been validated on synthetic
signals without spontaneous blinks, where it gave no false detection.

A command must not fire without the user meaning it, so the double blink is
**set aside as the trigger of `action`**, whatever the headset. `action` keeps
its meaning in the protocol ("grab", "interact": open a door, pick an object)
and the protocol does not change; only the gesture behind it is reconsidered.
Candidates, to be compared before choosing:

| Gesture | Signal | For | Against | Measurable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Double jaw clench | jaw muscle activity (EMG above 30 Hz), on every electrode at once | voluntary, strong, detectable on eight motor electrodes, used by consumer headsets | not a brain signal; talking, chewing and swallowing must not trigger it; the decoder pauses during the clench | with the headset (phase B) |
| Tongue motor imagery | brain activity (ERD), central | a brain signal, no artifact; a class of BNCI2014-001 | a fourth imagery class lowers the accuracy of the others | now, on public data |
| Both-hands imagery | brain activity, bilateral | a brain signal; a class of PhysionetMI | close to left and right | now, on public data |

Whatever the gesture, `action` acts only where it makes sense (in front of a
door: sandbox) and asks for a confirmation before acting on a real object.
Until the gesture is chosen, the double-blink detector stays in the code, but
must not drive a real object. The protocol schema, `docs/protocol.md` and the
README still describe `action` as a double blink: they will be updated once the
gesture is chosen.

## Resulting montages

With the double blink set aside, Fp1 loses its main purpose, and the eighth
channel of an 8-channel headset can go to the motor cortex:

| Headset | Decoding | Fp1 |
|---|---|---|
| 8 channels (IronBCI-8) | `FC3, FC4, C3, Cz, C4, CP3, CP4, CPz` | absent |
| 9 channels or more | the same eight | optional (eye artifacts, blink studies) |

The jaw clench study ([action-gesture.md](action-gesture.md)) shows that a
muscle gesture can be read on the motor electrodes alone. Before that, the
final choice for the IronBCI-8 waited for the `action` gesture: a jaw clench
or a motor imagery class works on the eight motor electrodes, while an eye
gesture would need Fp1 back. Models trained on eight electrodes cannot run on
seven, and the reverse.
